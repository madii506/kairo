Block 0, on the record. Block 1 is yours.

Kairo launches Solana coins on Raydium LaunchLab. The creator's buy rides inside the transaction that creates the coin, so block 0 opens with it. Kairo sends no other buy. From the next block, anyone can buy, and every coin prints a receipt of its block 0, read from the chain.

01What Kairo is

On most launchpads, the first block of a coin decides who owns it. In the same block the coin is created, a dev can buy with twenty wallets in one bundle while sniper bots buy the rest. The chart looks like real demand. By the time a person sees the coin, most of the supply has moved.

Kairo puts that block on the record. The creator gets one buy, inside the transaction that creates the coin, out in the open, before anyone else can buy. Kairo adds no bundle. If anyone else lands in block 0 anyway, the coin's receipt names them. From block 1, the coin is everyone's, on the same curve.

Where things stand. Launches are live on Raydium LaunchLab, next to the block 0 scanner and the simulator. Every coin launched through Kairo is listed in the app with its receipt.

02Block 0, explained

Solana produces a block roughly every 400 milliseconds. "Block 0" means the block a coin is created in: the very first moment it exists on chain.

A transaction can land in block 0 only if it was ready before anyone could see the coin. That's the creator, the creator's other wallets, or a bot paying a large tip to be packed in next to the creation.

Why it matters

  • Bundles. One person funds many fresh wallets and buys with all of them at once. On the chart it looks like a crowd. It's one hand holding a large share of the supply.
  • Snipes. Bots buy in the creation block, before a person can click, and sell into the first real buyers.
  • The price. Whoever is in block 0 buys at the lowest price the coin will ever have. Everyone after them pays more, for less.

03The three rules

Every Kairo launch follows the same three rules. There are no settings, no exceptions and no "trusted" wallets.

RULE 1Block 0 opens with one buy: the creator's, from the wallet that creates the coin, inside the same transaction.
RULE 2Kairo sends nothing else. No bundle, no second wallet. If anyone else lands in block 0, the receipt names them.
RULE 3From block 1, anyone can buy. The next block is open to everyone, on the same curve.
THE PROOFEvery coin prints a receipt of its block 0, and every line points to a transaction you can check on Solana.

04How a launch works

  1. Create. You name the coin, add an image and choose your buy in SOL. Kairo builds one transaction that creates the coin on Raydium LaunchLab and makes your buy, tries it against Solana, and your wallet signs it. The new coin's address signs too: its key is made in your browser and never leaves it.
  2. Block 0. The coin is born in that transaction, with your buy inside it. Nobody can buy before you, because the coin doesn't exist until your buy is already in it.
  3. Read back. Once Solana confirms, Kairo reads block 0 back from the chain. Any other wallet that bought in that block (a sniper, a second wallet) is named on the receipt with its share.
  4. Open. From block 1, about half a second later, anyone can buy. Everyone buys on the same curve, in the order the network sets.

Why block 0? Block 0 is the one block nobody outside can see coming, so it's where hidden wallets take the lowest price. Putting it on the record takes away the hiding place. From block 1, the coin exists in public and everyone can act on it.

A live receipt is stamped ON CHAIN and links to the launch on Solana, so you check the chain, not our word. Specimens and drafts are stamped as such.

07The block 0 scanner

The scanner reads any Solana coin's launch straight from the chain and replays block 0 through Kairo's rules. It works on pump.fun coins and on most other launches (Raydium, Meteora, PumpSwap and others).

What it reads

  • The launch block. It walks the coin's history back to its first transaction. For a pump.fun coin the creator comes from the coin's bonding curve. For other launches, trading starts the first time tokens land in a pool, and anything sent before that is reported as a pre-launch allocation.
  • Block 0. Every wallet that bought in that block. The creator's buy passes; every other wallet is flagged.
  • Bundles. One signer buying for several wallets, or one wallet that sent SOL to several early buyers just before they bought. Exchange hot wallets are ignored, because strangers withdrawing from the same exchange is not a bundle.
  • Block 1 onward. The first minute of open trading, for context. Those buys all pass.

How to read a verdict

  • Red: wallets besides the creator bought in block 0, or got the coin before trading opened.
  • Green: block 0 was the creator alone. A clean receipt.

Limits. The scanner reads up to 160 early transactions and checks the funding of up to 24 early wallets. Very busy or very old coins can have too many transactions to walk back in time on a public RPC; the scanner says so instead of guessing. When a read is partial, the result says "read in part".

08The simulator

The simulator runs one made-up launch twice with the same wallets: a dev buying 2% of supply, 20 bundle wallets, 8 snipers and 30 people. In the normal lane, the dev, the bundle and the snipers all land in block 0. In the clean lane, block 0 holds the dev's buy alone, the way a clean Kairo receipt reads, and everyone else buys from block 1 in the order the network sets.

Prices follow a simple bonding curve where the price rises with the share of supply already sold:

price(s) = 1 + 3s   ·   cost(s₀ → s₁) = (s₁ − s₀) + 1.5 (s₁² − s₀²)

The three results under the lanes come from that run and nothing else: the supply bought in block 0 besides the dev, the wallets in block 0 besides the dev, and what people paid per token next to the bots.

10What Kairo can't do

  • It can't stop a bot. Raydium LaunchLab has no way to close a block, so a sniper can still buy in block 0, next to the creator. What it can't do is buy before the creator, or hide: the receipt names it.
  • It can't pick the order inside a block. The network does.
  • It can't stop someone from buying with many wallets. Kairo never builds a bundle, and the scanner flags wallets with a shared funder wherever they sit.
  • It isn't a promise about price. A clean block 0 is not a good coin. Nothing here is financial advice.

11Safety and privacy

  • The site asks your wallet to sign one thing: the launch you build, after it was tried against Solana. It never asks for your recovery phrase.
  • The new coin's key is made in your browser, signs once and never leaves the page. The server stores the coin's image and metadata; it never holds a key or your SOL.
  • The scanner runs on our server and reads only public chain data. Nothing you paste is stored.
  • Your draft stays in your browser until you launch.
  • No cookies, no analytics, no trackers. Fonts are served from this site.

Block 0 is closed. Block 1 is yours.

Kairo is the launchpad without block 0. When a coin launches, the only buy in the block it's born in is the creator's. No bundles, no snipers, no second wallets. From the next block, anyone can buy.

01What Kairo is

On most launchpads, the first block of a coin decides who owns it. In the same block the coin is created, a dev can buy with twenty wallets in one bundle while sniper bots buy the rest. The chart looks like real demand. By the time a person sees the coin, most of the supply has moved.

Kairo takes that block off the table. The creator gets one buy, inside the transaction that creates the coin, out in the open. Nobody else can land in block 0. From block 1, the coin is everyone's, on the same curve.

Where things stand. The tools are live today: the block 0 scanner, the simulator and the launch form. Launches on Kairo open next, in Lap 2. There are no Kairo coins yet.

02Block 0, explained

Solana produces a block roughly every 400 milliseconds. "Block 0" means the block a coin is created in: the very first moment it exists on chain.

A transaction can land in block 0 only if it was ready before anyone could see the coin. That's the creator, the creator's other wallets, or a bot paying a large tip to be packed in next to the creation.

Why it matters

  • Bundles. One person funds many fresh wallets and buys with all of them at once. On the chart it looks like a crowd. It's one hand holding a large share of the supply.
  • Snipes. Bots buy in the creation block, before a person can click, and sell into the first real buyers.
  • The price. Whoever is in block 0 buys at the lowest price the coin will ever have. Everyone after them pays more, for less.

03The three rules

Every Kairo coin follows the same three rules. There are no settings, no exceptions and no "trusted" wallets.

RULE 1Block 0 is one buy: the creator's, from the wallet that creates the coin, in the same transaction.
RULE 2Nothing else lands in block 0. No bundles, no second wallets, no snipers, whoever pays the biggest tip.
RULE 3From block 1, anyone can buy. The next block is open to everyone, on the same curve.
THE PROOFEvery coin prints a receipt of its block 0, and every line points to a transaction you can check on Solana.

04How a launch works

  1. Create. You name the coin, add an image and choose your buy. Kairo builds one transaction that creates the coin and makes your buy, and your wallet signs it.
  2. Block 0. The coin is born in that transaction, with your buy inside it. That's block 0, and your buy is the only one in it.
  3. Bounced. Any other buy aimed at block 0 is refused: a bundle, a sniper, a second wallet of yours, whatever tip it pays.
  4. Open. From block 1, about half a second later, anyone can buy. Everyone buys on the same curve, in the order the network sets.

Why only block 0? Block 0 is the one block nobody outside can see coming. Closing it removes the hidden head start. From block 1, the coin exists in public and everyone can act on it.

06The receipt

Every Kairo coin carries a launch receipt. Here's what each line means.

COIN · VENUEThe coin's ticker and the chain it lives on.
BLOCK 0How many buys landed in the block the coin was born. On Kairo this is always one.
BUYERWho made that buy. On Kairo it's always the creator, from the wallet that created the coin.
SIZEHow much of the supply the creator's buy took.
BUNDLES · SNIPESOther wallets that bought in block 0. On Kairo both are zero.
BLOCK 1The first block open to everyone.

On a live Kairo coin, each line links to its transaction on Solana, so you check the chain, not our word. The receipts on this site are a specimen or your own draft, and their stamps say so.

07The block 0 scanner

The scanner reads any Solana coin's launch straight from the chain and replays block 0 through Kairo's rules. It works on pump.fun coins and on most other launches (Raydium, Meteora, PumpSwap and others).

What it reads

  • The launch block. It walks the coin's history back to its first transaction. For a pump.fun coin the creator comes from the coin's bonding curve. For other launches, trading starts the first time tokens land in a pool, and anything sent before that is reported as a pre-launch allocation.
  • Block 0. Every wallet that bought in that block. The creator's buy passes; every other wallet would have been turned away.
  • Bundles. One signer buying for several wallets, or one wallet that sent SOL to several early buyers just before they bought. Exchange hot wallets are ignored, because strangers withdrawing from the same exchange is not a bundle.
  • Block 1 onward. The first minute of open trading, for context. Those buys all pass.

How to read a verdict

  • Red: wallets besides the creator bought in block 0, or got the coin before trading opened. Kairo would have turned them away.
  • Green: block 0 was the creator alone. The launch already played by Kairo's rules.

Limits. The scanner reads up to 160 early transactions and checks the funding of up to 24 early wallets. Very busy or very old coins can have too many transactions to walk back in time on a public RPC; the scanner says so instead of guessing. When a read is partial, the result says "read in part".

08The simulator

The simulator runs one made-up launch twice with the same wallets: a dev buying 2% of supply, 20 bundle wallets, 8 snipers and 30 people. In the normal lane, the dev, the bundle and the snipers all land in block 0. In the Kairo lane, block 0 holds the dev's buy alone, and everyone else buys from block 1 in the order the network sets.

Prices follow a simple bonding curve where the price rises with the share of supply already sold:

price(s) = 1 + 3s   ·   cost(s₀ → s₁) = (s₁ − s₀) + 1.5 (s₁² − s₀²)

The three results under the lanes come from that run and nothing else: the supply bought in block 0 besides the dev, the wallets in block 0 besides the dev, and what people paid per token next to the bots.

10What Kairo can't do

  • It can't make bots disappear. From block 1, bots can buy like anyone. What they lose is block 0: no buying in the creation block next to the dev.
  • It can't pick the order inside a block. The network does.
  • It can't stop someone from buying with many wallets after block 0. It can stop those wallets from landing in block 0, and the scanner flags shared funders wherever they sit.
  • It isn't a promise about price. A clean block 0 is not a good coin. Nothing here is financial advice.

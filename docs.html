Not first. On time.

Kairo is the onchain anti-block-0 protocol. A coin's first block holds one buy, the creator's, and nothing else. Every coin opens for everyone at the same second, on the minute.

01What Kairo is

On most launchpads the first block of a coin decides who owns it. In the same block the coin is created, a dev can buy with twenty wallets in one bundle while sniper bots buy the rest. The chart looks like real demand. By the time a person sees the coin, most of the supply has moved.

Kairo takes that block off the table. The creator gets one buy, inside the transaction that creates the coin, out in the open. Nobody else can land in block 0. Then the coin waits for the next full minute and opens for everyone in the same second.

The name comes from kairos, the Greek word for the right moment. Kairo isn't about being first. It's about everyone arriving on time.

Where things stand. The tools are live today: the block 0 scanner, the openings board, the simulator and the receipt printer. Launches on Kairo come next, in Lap 2. Nothing on this site is a live Kairo coin yet.

02Block 0, explained

Solana produces a block roughly every 400 milliseconds. "Block 0" here means the block a coin is created in: the very first moment it exists on chain.

A transaction can land in block 0 only if it was ready before anyone could see the coin, which means it came from someone who knew the coin was coming. That's the creator, the creator's other wallets, or a bot watching the creator's wallet and paying a large tip to be first in line.

Why it matters

  • Bundles. One person funds many fresh wallets and buys with all of them at once. On the chart it looks like a crowd. It's one hand holding a large share of supply.
  • Snipes. Bots buy in the first block or two, before a person can click, and sell into the first real buyers.
  • The head start. Whoever is in block 0 buys at the lowest price the coin will ever have. Everyone after them pays more, for less.

03The three rules

Every Kairo coin follows the same three rules. There are no settings, no exceptions and no "trusted" wallets.

RULE 1Block 0 is one buy: the creator's, from the wallet that creates the coin, in the same transaction.
RULE 2Nothing else lands in block 0. No bundles, no second wallets, no snipes, whoever pays the biggest tip.
RULE 3Every coin opens on the minute. A public countdown to :00 (UTC), then trading opens for everyone in the same second.
THE PROOFEvery coin prints a receipt of its block 0, and every line points to a transaction you can check on Solana.

04How a coin opens

  1. Create. The creator launches the coin and, in the same transaction, makes one buy. That transaction is block 0.
  2. Closed. From that moment until the opening, every other buy is turned away. Bundles, snipers and second wallets all bounce.
  3. Countdown. The coin opens at the next full minute, :00 UTC. The second is public from the moment the coin exists, so everyone watches the same clock.
  4. Open. At :00, trading opens for everyone at once. Buys sent for the opening land in the same block or the next few, and the order inside a block is set by the network, not by who saw the coin first.

Why the minute? A random opening time would be a secret, and secrets leak to whoever is closest to the creator. A minute boundary is the most public time there is. Nobody can know it earlier than you, because everyone already knows it.

05The receipt

Every Kairo coin carries an opening receipt, printed the second it opens. Here is what each line means.

COIN · VENUEThe coin's ticker and the chain it lives on.
BLOCK 0How many buys landed in the block the coin was born. On Kairo this is always one.
BUYERWho made that buy. On Kairo it's always the creator, from the wallet that created the coin.
SIZEHow much of the supply the creator's buy took.
BUNDLES · SNIPESWallets that bought with the creator or ahead of the opening. On Kairo both are zero.
OPENS ATThe exact second trading opened, always on the minute, UTC.
BEFORE OPENTrades between block 0 and the opening. On Kairo this is zero.
OPENING BLOCKWho could buy at the opening: everyone.

On a live Kairo coin, each line links to its transaction on Solana, so you check the chain, not our word. The receipt on the home page and in the printer is a specimen or a draft, and says so on its stamp.

06The block 0 scanner

The scanner reads any Solana coin's launch straight from the chain and replays it through Kairo's rules. It works on pump.fun coins and on most other launches (Raydium, Meteora, PumpSwap and others).

What it reads

  • The launch block. It walks the coin's history back to its first transaction. For a pump.fun coin the creator comes from the coin's bonding curve; for other launches, trading starts the first time tokens land in a pool, and anything sent before that is reported as a pre-launch allocation.
  • Block 0. Every wallet that bought in that block. The creator's buy passes; every other wallet would have been turned away.
  • Before :00. Every buy after block 0 but before the next full minute. On Kairo those would have waited for the opening.
  • Bundles. One signer buying for several wallets, or one wallet that sent SOL to several early buyers just before they bought. Exchange hot wallets are ignored, because strangers withdrawing from the same exchange is not a bundle.

How to read a verdict

  • Red: wallets besides the creator bought in block 0, or got the coin before trading opened. Kairo would have turned them away.
  • Amber: block 0 was the creator alone, but buys landed before the next :00. Kairo would have held them for the opening.
  • Green: block 0 was the creator alone and nobody bought before the next :00. The launch already played by Kairo's rules.

Limits. The scanner reads up to 160 pre-opening transactions and checks the funding of up to 24 early wallets. Very busy or very old coins can have too many transactions to walk back in time on a public RPC; the scanner says so instead of guessing. When a read is partial, the result says "read in part".

07The openings board

The openings board in the app shows every new pump.fun coin as it's born, read live in your browser from PumpPortal's public feed. For each coin it shows when it was born, the creator's first buy, every buy that landed before the next :00, and a countdown to the second Kairo would have opened it. At every :00, every coin born in the minute before opens together.

  • BLOCK 0? appears when tokens left the curve in trades the feed never sent before its first trade. That usually means a bundle in block 0. It's a hint, not a verdict: press "Scan block 0" to read the coin's real block 0 from the chain.
  • The numbers at the top count only what happened while the page was open. Close the page and they start again.
  • If the feed goes down, the board says "Feed offline" and offers a retry. It never fills in made-up coins.

08The simulator

The simulator launches one made-up coin twice with the same wallets: a dev buying 2% of supply, 20 bundle wallets, 8 snipers and 30 people. In the normal lane, the dev, bundles and snipers all land in block 0 and people arrive over the next blocks. In the Kairo lane, the dev's one buy is block 0 and everyone else lands in the opening block, in an order the network picks.

Prices follow a simple bonding curve where the price rises with the share of supply already sold:

price(s) = 1 + 3s   ·   cost(s₀ → s₁) = (s₁ − s₀) + 1.5 (s₁² − s₀²)

The three results under the lanes come from that run and nothing else: the supply taken before the first person could buy, the bundle and sniper wallets in block 0, and what people paid per token next to the bots.

09The receipt printer

The receipt printer drafts the receipt your coin would carry on Kairo: the name, the ticker, the size of your one buy and the second it would open. Save it as a PNG or post it. It's a draft: nothing is created, signed or sent, and the stamp says DRAFT.

11What Kairo can't do

  • It can't make bots disappear. Bots can still buy at the opening, like anyone. What they lose is the head start: no block 0, nothing before :00.
  • It can't pick the order inside the opening block. The network does. Kairo makes sure that block is open to everyone at once.
  • It can't stop someone from using many wallets at the opening. It can stop those wallets from landing in block 0, and the scanner flags shared funders wherever they sit.
  • It isn't a promise about price. A fair start is not a good coin. Nothing here is financial advice.

12Safety and privacy

  • The site never asks you to connect a wallet, sign anything or send anything.
  • The scanner runs on our server and reads only public chain data. Nothing you paste is stored.
  • The openings board connects from your browser to PumpPortal's public feed and nowhere else.
  • No cookies, no analytics, no trackers. Fonts are served from this site.
